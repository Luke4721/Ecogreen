The E-Waste India Forgot. The Standard We Built.

India generates 1.75 million tonnes of electronic waste every year. Most of it disappears into an informal economy no auditor can trace.

This is the story of what happens to it — and the standard we built to make sure it happens properly.

It Began with a Refusal.

The story started in 2015, when e-waste in India was handled by an informal sector no one could trace. We refused to process a single device we could not account for. What looked like a business limitation became the foundation of everything we've built since.

A Decision That Reshaped E-Waste in India.

When we started, the entire sector ran on trust. No manifests, no serialised certificates, no downstream audits. Enterprise clients had no way to prove what had happened to their data.

We built the opposite. Every asset was photographed, weighed, scanned and logged the moment it entered our facility. Every gram that left was documented against a chain of custody an enterprise auditor could accept without follow-up.

That decision shaped everything that came after — our own collection network, NIST 800-88 Rev.1 certification, unannounced downstream audits. It was slower. It was more expensive. It was the only version worth doing.

The EcoGreen Way

Why We Call It a Closed Loop.

Because the material that comes out of your devices goes back into India — not into an export container, not into an open yard, not into a smelter someone signed off on without ever visiting.

Collect + Certify + Recover = Closed Loop

Every stage is documented. Every downstream refiner is audited. Every certificate is retrievable by serial number. Nothing is assumed.

Not disposal. Recovery. Not export. Domestic supply. Not trust. Proof.

SINCE 2015

A Decade Later, Still Refusing.

What started as a single collection route in Greater Noida is now a national network — five facilities, licensed collection partners in major metros, and a downstream refiner chain audited unannounced every quarter.

We have processed 60,142 metric tonnes of certified e-waste. We have saved an estimated 728.5 tCO₂e against primary mining. We have recovered 98% of the recoverable metals — and shipped every gram back into Indian manufacturing.

The scale changed. The refusal didn't.
